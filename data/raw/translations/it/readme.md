# Notes on the Italian translation

Italian translations of stars, constellation names, and DSO objects
are derived from:

- [Italian Wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/)
- [Stellarium](https://stellarium.org/)
- Ian Ridpath, Wil Tirion, _Guida delle Stelle e dei Pianeti_, Franco
  Muzzio Editore, 1988, ISBN 88-7413-071-6 (this is the Italian
  translation of _Guide to Stars and Planets_ by the Authors).
- _Universo--guida di viaggio_, Lonely Planet, 2019, ISBN
  978-88-5925-751-6.
- Patrick Moore, _Il Guinness dell'astronomia_, Biblioteca Universale
  Rizzoli, 1990, ISBN 88-17-11161-9.
- Giancarlo Favero, _Guida alla osservazione del cielo_, Arnoldo
  Mondadori Editore, 1984,

## Constellations

Most of the constellation names are standard. However, there are a
couple of special exceptions. One is the constellation _Bootes_, which
might be translated as _Boote_, _Bovaro_, or _Bifolco_. Here we use
_Boote_ since this is what Stellarium uses, as well as the Italian
association for amateur astronomers (UAI). The other exception is the
constellation _Norma_, which is sometimes translated as _Regolo_ or,
more rarely, _Squadra_. The most common translation seems to be
_Regolo_, so it is used here.

## Stars

There are just a handful of stars whose names are routinely translated
in Italian (i.e., _Regolo_, _Arturo_, _Sirio_, etc.).  All sources I
have consulted agree on the translations, so there should be no
problems here.

## Deep Sky Objects

DSOs are problematic. The scientific literature is all in English, so
there are no standard Italian translations. The names used here are
mostly taken from Stellarium or from the Italian Wikipedia.

Sometimes more than one translation is used, e.g., the Owl cluster
(NGC 457) might be referred to as _Ammasso Libellula_, _Ammasso
Civetta_ or _Ammasso Gufo_ (_civetta_ and _gufo_ are both translations
of "owl", although they refer to different birds); Stellarium prefers
_libellula_, so this is used.  Another example is "Brocchi's Cluster"
(Collinder 399), which is translated as _Ammasso di Brocchi_,
_Attaccapanni_, or _Appendiabiti_ (the latter two meaning
"coathanger"). Here se use _Ammasso di Brocchi_.

Where no Italian name is commonly used but the English denomination
has an obvious translation, the latter is used. For example, the
Running Man Nebula (NGC 1977) has been translated literally as
_Nebulosa Uomo che Corre_ although Stellarium calls it _Nebulosa
Running Man_ ("Nebulosa" == "Nebula").
